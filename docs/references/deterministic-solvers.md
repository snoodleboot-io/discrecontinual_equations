- **[Bashforth1883]** F. Bashforth, with J. C. Adams. *An Attempt to Test the Theories of Capillary Action by Comparing the Theoretical and Measured Forms of Drops of Fluid. With an Explanation of the Method of Integration Employed in Constructing the Tables which Give the Theoretical Forms of Such Drops, by J. C. Adams*. Cambridge University Press, 1883. Internet Archive `attempttotestthe00bashuoft`. Read: p. 18, Adams's step formula `y_1 - y_0 = w {q_0 + 1/2 Δq_0 + 5/12 Δ²q_0 + 3/8 Δ³q_0 + 251/720 Δ⁴q_0 + ...}`, with `q = dy/dt` and the differences taken over the values already computed. Stopping after `Δq_0` gives the Adams-Bashforth 2 weights `(3/2, -1/2)`; stopping after `Δ²q_0` gives the Adams-Bashforth 3 weights `(23/12, -16/12, 5/12)`. Used in: `adams_bashforth2_solver.AdamsBashforth2Solver.solve`, `adams_bashforth3_solver.AdamsBashforth3Solver.solve`.
- **[BrankinGladwellShampine1991]** R. W. Brankin, I. Gladwell, L. F. Shampine. *RKSUITE: a suite of Runge-Kutta codes for the initial value problem for ODEs*, Release 1.0, November 1991. Netlib, `ode/rksuite/rksuite.f` and `rksuite.doc`. Read: subroutine `CONST`, `METHD = 3`, the Prince-Dormand 13-stage pair to 30 digits, which notes that "BHAT(*) ... [is] the formula used to advance the integration, here the one of order 8" and "B(*) ... the one of order 7". This is a secondary source; we used it to check [PrinceDormand1981] digit by digit. It shows that the repository's eighth-order weight `b8[7] = 561292735/797845732` should be `561292985/797845732`, and that the repository's `b9` weights are the seventh-order ones. Used in: `dormand_prince89_solver.DormandPrince89Solver`.
- **[CashKarp1990]** J. R. Cash, A. H. Karp. *A variable order Runge-Kutta method for initial value problems with rapidly varying right-hand sides*. ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 16(3):201-222, 1990. DOI: 10.1145/79505.79507. We did not read the paper: the publisher refused automated access. The bibliographic data comes from Crossref. The tableau was checked against the Wikipedia article *Cash-Karp method* (accessed 2026-11-24), which says the first weight row `(37/378, ..., 512/1771)` is fifth order. We also solved the order conditions exactly, which agrees. The repository labels that row `b4` and propagates the other, fourth-order, row. Used in: `cash_karp_solver.CashKarpSolver`.
- **[DormandPrince1980]** J. R. Dormand, P. J. Prince. *A family of embedded Runge-Kutta formulae*. Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics 6(1):19-26, 1980. DOI: 10.1016/0771-050X(80)90013-3. We did not read the paper: the publisher refused automated access. The bibliographic data comes from Crossref. The seven-stage 5(4) tableau was checked against [HairerWanner1996], whose routine `CDOPRI` gives "Runge-Kutta coefficients of Dormand and Prince (1980)", and against the Wikipedia article *Dormand-Prince method* (accessed 2026-11-24). We also solved the order conditions exactly. Every `a_ij` and `b_i` agrees, but the repository's node `c_5 = 7/9` should be `8/9` (`C5=8.D0/9.D0` in `CDOPRI`, and the row sum of `a_5j`). Used in: `dormand_prince_solver.DormandPrinceSolver`.
- **[Euler1768]** L. Euler. *Institutionum calculi integralis*, vol. 1. St. Petersburg, 1768 (Eneström E342). Read in I. Bruce's English translation, *Concerning the approximate integration of differential equations*, part I, section II, chapter VII, at 17centurymaths.com (`contents/euler/intcalvol1/part2ch7.pdf`), which reprints the Latin alongside. Problem 85, section 650: with `dy/dx = V`, set `V = A` at `(a, b)` and step to `b' = b + A (a' - a)`, then repeat. Sections 652-653: the per-step errors add up, and they are largest where `V` varies most. The repository evaluates `V` at the new abscissa `a'` and the old `y`, not at `(a, b)`. Used in: `euler_solver.EulerSolver.solve`.
- **[Fehlberg1969]** E. Fehlberg. *Low-order classical Runge-Kutta formulas with stepsize control and their application to some heat transfer problems*. NASA Technical Report R-315, George C. Marshall Space Flight Center, July 1969. NTRS 19690021375. Read: eqs. (2)-(3), the formula pair (a fourth-order `y` to advance, a fifth-order `ŷ` whose difference from `y` estimates the error), and Table III, "Coefficients for RK4(5), Formula 2" (p. 13). All of the repository's coefficients match Table III. The repository advances with the fifth-order `ŷ`, not Fehlberg's fourth-order `y`. The code's docstring cited Fehlberg's NASA TR R-287 (1968), whose title names fifth- to eighth-order formulas; the coefficients used are those of R-315. Used in: `fehlberg_solver.FehlbergSolver`.
- **[HairerNorsettWanner1993]** E. Hairer, S. P. Nørsett, G. Wanner. *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems*, 2nd ed. Springer Series in Computational Mathematics 8, Springer, 1993. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-540-78862-1. §III.1: the Adams formulas on the equidistant grid `t_n = t_0 + n h`. Not read directly; cited through the Wikipedia article *Linear multistep method* (accessed 2026-11-25), which defines the grid that way and gives the two-step formula `y_{n+2} = y_{n+1} + (3/2) h f_{n+1} - (1/2) h f_n`. Used in: `solver_config.FixedStepConfig.times` (why a partial last step is refused rather than clipped: the Adams-Bashforth 2 and 3 coefficients assume a constant step).
- **[HairerWanner1996]** E. Hairer, G. Wanner. *DOPRI5*, Fortran code, version of April 25, 1996 (last correction August 8, 2005). Its header says it is "described in" Hairer, Nørsett and Wanner, *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1993. Read: the copy vendored in SciPy v1.10.0, `scipy/integrate/dop/dopri5.f`. Routine `CDOPRI` gives the coefficients. Routine `DOPCOR` gives the step-size control: the error norm `ERR = sqrt(mean((err_i/sk_i)^2))` with `sk_i = atol + rtol max(|y_i|, |y1_i|)`, then `FAC11 = ERR**EXPO1` with `EXPO1 = 0.2 - 0.75 BETA`, then `FAC = FAC11 / FACOLD**BETA` ("Lund-stabilization"), with `BETA` defaulting to 0.04, `SAFE` to 0.9 and the ratio of new to old step held in `[0.2, 10]`. The repository takes its `safety_factor = 0.9` and `beta = 0.04` defaults from here, but its controller is `h S err^-(1+beta)/q`, which uses no previous error. Used in: `dormand_prince_solver`, `cash_karp_solver`, `fehlberg_solver`, `dormand_prince89_solver` (`_compute_new_step_size`, `_accept_step`).
- **[NumPyDevelopers2026]** NumPy Developers. *numpy.arange*. NumPy Reference, stable, accessed 2026-11-25. https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.arange.html. The length of a float `arange` is `ceil((stop - start)/step)`, which "may result in the last element of out being greater than stop", and "the length of the output might not be numerically stable". This is the defect DEQ-129 replaces. Used in: `solver_config.uniform_step_count`, `solver_config.uniform_grid`.
- **[PrinceDormand1981]** P. J. Prince, J. R. Dormand. *High order embedded Runge-Kutta formulae*. Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics 7(1):67-75, 1981. DOI: 10.1016/0771-050X(81)90010-3. We did not read the paper: the publisher refused automated access. The bibliographic data comes from Crossref. The thirteen-stage 8(7) tableau was checked against [BrankinGladwellShampine1991], which cites this paper for it, and by solving the order conditions in exact rational arithmetic. Every `a_ij`, every `c_i` and the seventh-order weights agree to 1e-16. One eighth-order weight differs in its fourth significant digit (see [BrankinGladwellShampine1991]). The pair is 8(7), not the 8(9) the class names. The code's docstring cited Dormand and Prince, "Runge-Kutta triples" (1987). We did not check that paper; RKSUITE attributes this tableau to the 1981 paper. Used in: `dormand_prince89_solver.DormandPrince89Solver`.
- **[PythonDocs2026]** Python Software Foundation. *Floating-Point Arithmetic: Issues and Limitations*. The Python Tutorial, ch. 15, accessed 2026-11-25. https://docs.python.org/3/tutorial/floatingpoint.html. 0.1 is not a binary fraction, and `0.1 + 0.1 + 0.1 == 0.3` is `False`, so a decimal span is a multiple of a decimal step only up to rounding. Used in: `solver_config.GRID_TOLERANCE`.
