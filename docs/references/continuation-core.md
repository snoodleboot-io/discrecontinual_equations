- **[AllgowerGeorg1990]** E. L. Allgower and K. Georg. *Numerical Continuation Methods: An Introduction*. Springer Series in Computational Mathematics 13, Springer, 1990. DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-61257-2 (reprinted as *Introduction to Numerical Continuation Methods*, SIAM Classics in Applied Mathematics 45, 2003, DOI 10.1137/1.9780898719154). Used in: `predictor.TangentPredictor` (Euler predictor, eq. (2.2.2)); `linear_solver.LeastSquaresFallbackSolver` (the augmented Jacobian (2.1.5), nonsingular along a regular curve; the Moore-Penrose step of Lemma (3.2.3)); `tangent.BorderedTangentComputer` (the tangent induced by a matrix, Definition (2.1.7)); `step_controller.AdaptiveStepController` (control by corrector iteration count, Algorithm (6.2.1)); `localizer.RefiningLocalizer` and `root_finder` (zero points of a functional along the curve, Section 9.2, eq. (9.2.1)); `switching.NullSpaceBranchSwitcher` (simple bifurcation points, Definition (8.1.11) and Lemma (8.1.13); branching off via the bifurcation equation, Section 8.3: the kernel and left-kernel vectors (8.3.3)–(8.3.5), the coefficient matrix (8.3.7) by the difference formulae (8.3.8)–(8.3.9), and the quadratic (8.3.11) for the tangents). The failure of parameter stepping at turning points is their Algorithm (1.6) and Figure 1.b. Read from the 1990 edition's text; the SIAM reprint's numbering was not checked. For the switcher, the notes suggest a difference step of the cube root of machine precision for (8.3.9), justified by (10.3.24)–(10.3.27); that justification is for a first difference, so the switcher uses the fourth root, which balances a second difference's truncation and cancellation errors.
